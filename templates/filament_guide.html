The whole system in four sentences

  1. A filament is a pool. "White PLA" is one entry no matter how many spools you own.
  2. You buy spools into it — how many, and what you paid for one.
  3. You pick the colour on the Job Calculator, per job, because the customer decides the colour — not the product.
  4. Once a month you weigh what is really there and hit Reconcile, because the numbers drift.
One SKU, any colour SGN-001 is the Sign Holder whether it prints white for one customer or blue for the next, regardless of which department sells it. The product page holds the recipe — grams, plate quantity, print time — and never a colour. That is why you never end up with SGN-001-WHITE and SGN-001-BLUE.
Why grams and not spools A print does not use "half a spool," it uses 240 g. If the system counted spools it could never subtract a print. Grams are the unit that works; spools are shown alongside so the number still means something when you look at the shelf.

1Add a filament

Top of the Filament page. You need two things: Material and Color.

Material
PLA, PLA+, PETG, TPU, PLA Silk. Pick from the list or type your own — the list is there to stop typos, not to limit you.
Color
What you call it. "White." "Matte Charcoal." Whatever you will recognise in a dropdown.
Brand
Optional, but it matters if Bambu white and Sunlu white behave differently or cost differently. Leave it blank and they share one pool.
Swatch
Purely visual. Makes the list scannable at a glance.
Spool Size (g)
Defaults to 1000 because that is what you buy. Change it for refills, 750 g spools, or a 5 kg roll.
Reorder At (g)
When stock drops to this, the row flags. 500 g is half a spool — enough warning to order before you are stuck mid-job.
Spools You Have / Price per Spool
Optional, and the shortcut worth using. Fill these in and your opening stock is logged in the same click — no second step.
One entry per material + color + brand The system refuses a duplicate on purpose. Three "white PLA" pools would make every stock number and every reorder alert meaningless.

2Buy spools into it

Hit + Spools on any row.

  • Spools — how many you bought. Usually 1.
  • Grams per Spool — pre-filled from the filament's spool size. Only change it if this particular purchase is different.
  • Price per Spool — what one spool cost. Bought 2 at $21.99 each? Spools 2, price 21.99. Not $43.98.
  • Note — where you bought it, or anything you want to remember.
This is where your cost number comes from The $ / Spool and $ / g columns are a weighted average of everything you have actually paid. Buy cheap once and expensive once, and the average sits between them — weighted by grams, not by number of purchases. Skip the price and stock still tracks fine, you just get a dash instead of a cost.

3Keep it honest

The Adjust button holds two different corrections. They are not the same thing.

Log Waste

Filament you burned with nothing sellable to show for it. A print that failed at 60%. A nozzle clog you cleared. Purge you did not account for. Comes straight off stock.

Log Personal Print

Something you printed for yourself. Enter the grams and what it was. Stock comes down exactly like a customer job, but nothing is costed and no job is created — there is no customer and no price. Printed it in two colours? Log each one on its own filament.

Reconcile

Put the spools on a kitchen scale, subtract the empty spool weight, and enter what it actually reads. The system logs the difference and moves on. No blame, no unpicking where it went.

Do this monthly. It is not optional. Bambu Studio tells you the filament used for the model. It does not count the purge tower, AMS filament changes, skirt, brim, or the last few metres you cannot print with. On multi-colour work purge alone can be a quarter of the job. Deduct only the sliced estimate and after fifteen spools the system says 3,400 g and your shelf says 2,600 g. Reconcile is what stops that.

Reading the table

ColumnWhat it means
On HandGrams left. The bar is a rough fill gauge against three spools' worth.
SpoolsThe same number divided by spool size. 1.73 means about one and three-quarter spools.
$ / SpoolWeighted average of what you have paid per spool.
$ / gThe same figure per gram. This is the number job costing will use.
ValueGrams on hand × cost per gram. What is sitting on your shelf in dollars.
StatusIn stock above threshold, Reorder at or below it, Out at zero or less.

Ledger on any row shows every gram in and out, newest first, with a running balance. That is the audit trail — if a number ever looks wrong, the answer is in there.

4Cost a job

Job Calculator → pick the SKU → a Filament for This Job dropdown appears for every printed part. Choose the colour the customer wants. Two-part product in two colours? Pick each one separately.

  • Cost is calculated from what those exact spools cost you — blue at $27.99 prices differently from white at $21.99.
  • Saving the job draws those grams out of stock, including the spare units on a partly-filled plate. A 40-unit order of a 12-up part is 4 runs, so 4 plates of filament come off.
  • Deleting a job from Job Cost History puts the filament back.
  • Leave a part unpicked and it falls back to the catalog's rough figure and moves no stock.

One part, two colours

Different from a two-part product. A sign face printed blue with red lettering is one printed piece using two filaments — the AMS swaps mid-print. Hit + colour under that part's filament and a second row appears.

  • Once there are two or more colours, each gets its own grams box. Enter what Bambu Studio reports for each filament — 140 g blue, 60 g red.
  • The Filament (g) column then becomes a read-only total of those figures, so you never type the same number twice.
  • Cost is the sum of each colour at its own rate, so a cheap blue and an expensive red price correctly rather than averaging.
  • Stock comes off each pool separately. Three runs of a 140/60 part draws 420 g blue and 180 g red.
  • One colour? Do nothing — the grams box stays hidden and the part works exactly as before.

If you do nothing else

  1. Add every filament you own, with spool count and price, one time.
  2. Every time you buy, hit + Spools. Ten seconds.
  3. When you quote a job, pick the colour for each part — + colour if one part uses two.
  4. Printing for yourself? Adjust → Log Personal Print. Grams and what it was.
  5. Once a month, weigh everything and Reconcile.

That is the whole system. Everything else on these pages is reporting on top of those four habits.
